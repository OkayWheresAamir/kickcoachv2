    Filming it

    1. Side-on to the kick.Camera level with the ball, at right angles to where the ball goes. Front-on or angled clips are refused.
    2. Keep it still.On a tripod or propped up, at about hip height.
    3. Whole body and ball in viewfrom the last steps of the run-up to the follow-through.
    4. One player, one kick.Trim the clip to a few seconds around the kick.
    5. Kicking leg nearer the cameraif you can; the far leg is often hidden.

      How it works

      1. Pose. RTMPose finds 23 body and foot keypoints in each frame (chosen over MediaPipe and MoveNet on hand-labelled frames).
      2. Events. Ball contact is the last frame before a ball detector sees the ball move; the backswing is the knee's most bent point before it.
      3. Measurements. Angles come from the cleaned keypoints with plain geometry. Any value outside a sanity bound is reported as not measured, never clamped.
      4. Comparison. Each value is set against norms extracted from biomechanics papers and checked by hand, with the study's population and caveats.
      5. Feedback. Fault rules with cited or clearly marked heuristic thresholds pick what to work on. The wording comes from an LLM under a validator, or from templates.

      Every measurement is evaluated against human labels, with v1 and a frontier video model as baselines. See the results.

      Your video is deleted as soon as it has been analysed.

      KickCoach measures one kind of kick from 2D video. It isn't a substitute for a coach, and lab studies used 3D capture on small samples.
